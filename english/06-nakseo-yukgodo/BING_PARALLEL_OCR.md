# Bing-Section Parallel OCR: Three-Dimensional Counting Rods and 六觚

## Source record

This document preserves a Bing-section OCR directly transcribed by the user from the original. No reliable printed transcription of this passage is currently stored in the repository or recoverable through public search. The OCR is therefore retained as the primary working source; no automatic character emendation is applied.

The complete verbatim OCR is preserved in the [Korean source record](../../korean/06-nakseo-yukgodo/BING_PARALLEL_OCR.md). The following is the punctuated working layout of the passage directly parallel to Yukgodo. It changes lineation only, not glyphs:

```text
今有三積算子一束，外周九十隻，問中積者。
凢三積，每十八隻，包中六外，成六觚。
置九千隻，添六隻，以十二而一，得八隻；自乗，得六十四隻；又六囙之。
○一法：置周加六隻，得數即一之；又列加得之數，以十二而……
```

The surrounding OCR also contains iterative-calculation passages with `循次遍加`, `倍加`, `添一個/添二個/添半個`, `以六歸之`, and `以三歸之`. These are retained in the Korean source record without glyph-level normalization.

## Consequence for the reading

This parallel passage materially strengthens three points:

1. `包` is not an isolated OCR accident: it occurs in the construction vocabulary for a rod bundle that "forms a 六觚."
2. `隻` repeatedly counts perimeter, units, and squared results in a rod-count calculation. Its relation to the single-member structure of the vacant-center miniature remains a separate, additional line of evidence.
3. The Yukgodo Naejeok Method is much more securely placed within a Bing-section tradition of hexagonal count/area computation using perimeter, increments, division, powers, and multiples, rather than a direct value-placement algorithm.

The passage still does not supply a literal Yukgodo instruction such as `對觚相合二百七十一`. It therefore does not promote the 271 antipodal-complement rule to direct transcription. It is strong independent parallel evidence for the **geometric/counting** interpretation of the Naejeok Method.
