### Summary of the Criteria for Each Row (Property)

* **Latin square / Orthogonal Latin squares**: In the original data, if even a single cell is out of place so that a number repeats in any row or column, the diagram was marked **X**. Accordingly, only the separately recorded Baekjajasuyin-yang-chakjong and Baekjamosuyin-yang-chakjong (base) receive an **O**. (However, although this particular pair of Latin squares in the original is not itself perfectly mutually orthogonal, Choi Seok-jeong clearly presented the methodology for combining the two, so they are marked in recognition of the soundness of the combining principle itself and the historical significance of this combinatorial discovery.)
* **Magic square**: The only diagrams whose row, column, and diagonal sums converge perfectly are **Yukyukdo** and **Gusudo**, which contain no misprints.
* **Nested grids (compound magic square)**: **Gusudo** is the only diagram with a structure in which smaller magic squares inside the overall magic square maintain their consistency as blocks.
* **Point symmetry / Line symmetry**: Even where misprints exist, this is the property that the symmetry axis of the overall grid remains perfectly mirror-consistent in terms of topology (positional arrangement). `Sunsu-do` preserves point symmetry, `Gyosu-do` preserves line symmetry (top-bottom reflection), and `Baekjado` has a left-right axis of symmetry (line symmetry).
* **Complete permutation of 1–100**: Whether every card from $1$ to $100$ is used exactly once, with no duplication or omission. Among the order-ten Hundred-Numbers family, only **Baekjayin-yang-jamo-chakjong** and **Baekjado**, which have no misprints, earn an **O**.

### Summary of the Corrected-Version Criteria for Each Row (Property)

* **Latin square / Orthogonal Latin squares**: Since the corrected versions have all misprints fixed, the three diagrams of the order-ten Hundred-Numbers family (`Sunsu-do`, `Gyosu-do`, `Jamo-chakjong-do`) satisfy both the Latin-square property that no number repeats in any row or column, and the orthogonality property that the two yin and yang bases cross perfectly (**O**). `Baekjajasuyin-yang-chakjong` does not include the combined square; only the pair of two base Latin squares is recorded separately, and it is marked to indicate that the orthogonality and the theoretical soundness of the combining methodology hold.
* **Magic square**: The corrected versions are adjusted so that the numbers from 1 to 100 form a flawless permutation and every row, column, and main diagonal sums to 505. Accordingly, `Sunsu-do`, `Gyosu-do`, and `Jamo-chakjong-do` are also classified as complete magic squares (**O**). `Baekjado` has row and column sums of 505 but its diagonal sums do not match, so it is classified only as semi-magic.
* **Yukyukdo / Gusudo**: Since the originals themselves contain no misprints, they retain their consistency as-is and are classified as perfect magic squares (**O**). However, because they are not formed by combining a pair of algebraic bases, they are excluded from the Latin and orthogonal square classifications (**X**).
