## Properties of the Major *Gusuryak* Diagrams

| Diagram | Latin square | Orthogonal Latin squares | Magic square | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Yukyukdo | X | X | O | Normal 1–36 magic square |
| Gusudo | X | X | O | Normal 1–81 compound magic square |
| Baekjajasuyin-yang-chakjong | O | O | X | Yin and yang bases recorded separately |
| Baekjasaengseong-sunsu | O | 50-orthogonal | X | Ordered-pair grid; 180° rotational symmetry |
| Baekjasaengseong-gyosu | O | 50-orthogonal | X | Ordered-pair grid; 1–100 test is not applicable |
| Baekjayin-yang-jamo-chakjong | X | X | X | Complete 1–100 permutation |
| Baekjado | X | X | X | Semi-magic square |

### Classification of Baekjasaengseong-gyosu

Gyosu-do is an ordered-pair grid: every cell has the form `(a,b)`. It is a **50-orthogonal Latin-square pair** of order ten: 50 ordered pairs each occur twice. It must not be collapsed into a decimal 1–100 array, and numerical substitutions such as `16→11` or `85→86` are not applied. The diagram is not a magic square.

Symmetry classifications belonging to numerical arrays must not be transferred to Gyosu-do.

### Classification of Baekjasaengseong-sunsu

Sunsu-do is likewise an ordered-pair grid, not a two-digit number array.
Read `10` as `(0,10)`, a one-digit `7` as `(0,7)`, and every other two-digit
entry by splitting its digits. Both components are order-ten Latin squares and
their superposition contains 50 ordered pairs twice each. Its 180-degree
rotation maps every pair to the same pair at the antipodal cell.
