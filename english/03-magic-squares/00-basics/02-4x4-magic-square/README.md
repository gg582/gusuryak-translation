## Yang Square (陽圖)

```
2 ,16,13, 3
11, 5, 8,10
7, 9,12, 6
14, 4, 1,15
```

## Yin Square (陰圖)

```
4, 9, 5,16
14, 7,11, 2
15, 6,10, 3
1,12, 8,13
```

Let us superimpose the two.

( 2, 4)(16, 9)(13, 5)( 3,16)
(11,14)( 5, 7)( 8,11)(10, 2)
( 7,15)( 9, 6)(12,10)( 6, 3)
(14, 1)( 4,12)( 1, 8)(15,13)

十六子順排四行 倒排四行
The sixteen numbers are arranged in four rows in the forward direction and in four rows in the reverse direction.

順排者先以 外四角對換得以
In the forward arrangement, first the outer four corners are swapped with their diagonally opposite positions.

內四角對換得陰圖
Then the inner four corners are swapped with their diagonally opposite positions to obtain the yin square.

橫斜直皆三十四數
Horizontally, vertically, and diagonally, every line yields thirty-four (皆三十四數).

一變以爲陽圖
With one transformation, the yang square is obtained as well.

## Four-Row Arrangement

```
1, 2, 15, 16 
3, 4, 13, 14
5, 6, 11, 12
7, 8, 9, 10
```

法曰以數分
The method says: divide the numbers,

兩行
into two rows,

| Sasado (四四圖) | 一 | 二 | 三 | 四 | 五 | 六 | 七 | 八 |
| ------ | - | - | - | - | - | - | - | - | 
| Eumdo (陰圖) | 十六 | 十五 | 十四 | 十三 | 十二 | 十一 | 十 | 九 |

二子皆等十七數
The sum of each pair of numbers is equally seventeen.

又分四行而橫行先等三十四數
Dividing these again into four rows, the horizontal rows first all become equal at thirty-four.

## Reference

A famous artistic treatment of this 4×4 magic-square construction algorithm is engraved in a copperplate engraving by Albrecht Dürer, dated 1514 (*Melencolia I*).
In the West, this algorithm is called "Dürer's algorithm" in his honor.

Among the systematic prior studies of magic squares (縱橫圖), the most deeply rooted is the *Yang Hui Suanfa* (楊輝算法), the mathematical work of Yang Hui of the Southern Song dynasty, whose name is still well known today.
It was written around 1275, and it is very likely a text that Choi Seok-jeong consulted.

## Translator's Note

Research on orthogonal Latin squares and on puzzles with shared vertices (such as the Jisu-guimun-do) was quite early in Korea, but for general magic squares many renowned treatises came out of China and Europe.

What is remarkable is that, despite this back-and-forth taking of the lead, the cultural development of East and West proceeds at a similar pace, as if synchronized around certain points in time.

It brings to mind the synchronicity of the *Baki* series.
