# Commentary

If the mother-number diagrams (母數圖) of 07 and 08 are diagrams that place the ordered pairs (a, b), then the child-number diagram (子數圖) of 09 is a diagram that places in each cell their product a×b, that is, the nine-nine number (九九數). Just as the mother-number diagrams make 90 in every row and column, the child-number diagrams make 225 in every row and column. The 81 cells contain the 81 products of the nine-nine multiplication table (one for each ordered pair (a,b)) exactly once each — literally **九九八十一** (nine-nine eighty-one).

Below are the results of verifying the two diagrams and analyzing their structure (`../analyze_zisu.py`, `../visualize_zisu.py`).

## Common properties

- The 81 cells hold the 81 products of the nine-nine table exactly once for each ordered pair (a,b) (down to the details that 1 occurs only as 1×1, and 25, 49, 64, and 81 each occur only once).
- Every row and column sums to 225, and the grand total is 2025 = 45².
- The product sum 225 corresponds to the (a+b) sum 90 of the mother-number diagrams. When the nine pairs of a row use each of 1–9 once in each component, the sum of products varies between 165 and 285 depending on the arrangement; in both diagrams every row is set evenly at the median value 225. Incidentally, the two diagonal sums of the yin diagram are exactly the two extremes 165 and 285.

## Yang diagram: the Kronecker product of the Luoshu

The yang diagram is **Luoshu⊗Luoshu**. For the Luoshu L = (4 9 2 / 3 5 7 / 8 1 6), the value of each cell is **L(position of the palace) × L(position within the palace)**. In other words, the nine palaces (3×3) are each k×Luoshu (k = 1,2,…,9), and the arrangement of the multiples k is again the Luoshu. For example, the central palace is 5×Luoshu, and the bottom-middle palace is the Luoshu itself (k = 1).

- Each palace is a small magic square in its own right. The sums of the three-cell sub-rows and sub-columns within a palace are all equal to 15k.
- The arrangement of the palace constants (15k), 60 135 30 / 45 75 105 / 120 15 90, is again 15×Luoshu, and its row, column, and diagonal sums are 225.
- Therefore not only the rows and columns but **both diagonals also sum to 225** — the 09 yang diagram is a complete 9×9 magic square, diagonals included.
- Since the ordered pair (Luoshu value of the palace, Luoshu value within the palace) at each cell passes through the 81 ordered pairs exactly once without duplication, the fact that the 81 products each appear once is also a direct consequence of this structure.
- The placement rule of the pairs belongs to a different family from that of the 07 yang diagram (a Latin square in which each component is a permutation of 1–9 across rows and columns).

## Yin diagram: anti-diagonal symmetry

Instead of the palace-level rule of the yang diagram, the yin diagram is organized by **anti-diagonal (upper-right ↔ lower-left) symmetry**. For the cells above, cell(r,c) = cell(8−c,8−r) holds.

- On the anti-diagonal lie the nine squares 1², 8², 4², 3², 5², 7², 6², 2², 9². That is, all the diagonal pairs (k,k) lie on this axis.
- All rows and columns sum to 225, but the diagonals do not. The anti-diagonal sum is 285, the sum of the squares; the main diagonal sum is 165 (9, 21, 24, 16, 25, 16, 24, 21, 9 — a palindrome), and the average of the two is 225.
- The palace sums 152 160 363 / 280 235 160 / 243 280 152 are likewise equal within anti-diagonally symmetric pairs (152=152, 160=160, 280=280).
- This is an arrangement of the same character as the **尨妙** — seemingly jumbled yet mysteriously balanced — that the postscript of the 08 yin diagram spoke of.

## Overlap of the yang and yin diagrams

- There are six cells where the same value sits at the same position: row 2 column 7 value 6, row 3 column 3 value 24, row 3 column 7 value 16, the center 25, row 7 column 7 value 24, and row 8 column 3 value 56. Just as the fixed point in the 07·08 overlap was the single center (5,5), here too the center 25 = 5×5 is shared by the yang and yin diagrams.
- The yang diagram is a regular arrangement generated by the self-product of the Luoshu, while the yin diagram is a mixed arrangement that reorders the same 81 products by anti-diagonal symmetry; this corresponds to the complementary relationship between 07 (row–column Latin) and 08 (palace Latin).
