## 數位苐三 Place Value and Notation of Numbers: Chapter 3
Suwi (數位) No. 3 (The Place Value and Notation of Numbers)

位者數之溥也所以循次而正數者延有名斯有位矣故數位次之
A place (位) is the spread-out expanse of numbers. Because numbers are set in proper order by following the sequence — where there is a name, a place comes into being — place value (數位) comes next.

小數借散數紀位尺度同七寸下為小數
Small numbers (小數) borrow the scattered numbers (散數, dispersed/small-unit numbers) to record the places, the same as in linear measure (尺度); the places below 7 chon (寸) become small numbers.

借凢數紀位量𢖍
Borrowing ordinary numbers (凡數) to record the places applies to volume (量) and weight (衡).

六㣲十纎為微九毫
6 mi (微): ten seom (纖) make one mi (微); this is 9 ho (毫).

三忽十㣲為忽八整
3 hol (忽): ten mi (微) make one hol (忽); this is 8 jeong (整).

五絲十忽為絲七分
5 sa (絲): ten hol (忽) make one sa (絲); this is 7 bun (分).

九毫十絲為毫六寸
9 ho (毫): ten sa (絲) make one ho (毫); this is 6 chon (寸).

四釐十毫為釐五尺
4 ri (釐): ten ho (毫) make one ri (釐); this is 5 chi (尺).

二分十一為文分
2 bun (分): ten ri make one bun (分); this is the place of munbun (文分).

釐為分四十
That the ri (釐) becomes the bun (分) corresponds to the 40 place.

一右即單數下細分之
The above (右) is the finer subdivision below the single-unit numbers (單數).

數列於正數單位之二百
The numbers are arrayed below the units of the whole numbers (正數).

右如尺下之寸分千二千下之升合也
The above is like the chon (寸) and bun (分) below the chi (尺), or the seung (升) and hap (合) below the du (斗).

正數紀位
The places are recorded with whole numbers (正數).

借經世萬物之數一萬
Borrowing the "number of the myriad things" from the Jingse (經世), 10,000.

六即單位斛量斗下為小數
6 is the unit place; for grain measures by the hu (斛), the places below the du (斗) become small numbers.

七十一勺
71 jak (勺).

五百六合
506 hap (合).

六千二升
6,002 seung (升).

一萬七斗
10,007 du (斗).

八十即十萬位五石
80, that is, 5 seok (石) at the hundred-thousands place.

九百即百萬位三十
900, that is, 30 at the millions place.

八千即千萬位八百
8,000, that is, 800 at the ten-millions place.

二萬即萬萬位四千
是為億數
20,000, that is, 4,000 at the wanwan (萬萬, myriad-myriad) place.
This is a number of the eok order (億數).

大數
Large Numbers (大數)

借經世元㑹之數紀位九萬
Borrowing the numbers of the Jingse Yuanhui (經世元㑹), the places are recorded with 90,000.

六億權𢖍錢下為小數
600 million; in the weight units (權衡), the places below the jeon (錢, mace) become small numbers.

五十即十億位一毫
50, that is, 1 ho (毫) at the ten-eok (billions) place.

四百即百億位二釐
400, that is, 2 ri (釐) at the hundred-eok (ten-billions) place.

七千即千億位三分
7,000, that is, 3 bun (分) at the thousand-eok (hundred-billions) place.

○萬即萬億位四錢
10,000, that is, 4 jeon (錢) at the wan-eok (trillions) place.

九十即十萬億位五两
90, that is, 5 ryang (兩, tael) at the ten-wan-eok place.

九百即百萬億位六十
900, that is, 60 at the hundred-wan-eok place.

○即千萬億位七百
—, that is, 700 at the thousand-wan-eok place.

一兆即萬萬億位 是為兆數八千
1 jo (兆), that is, the wanwan-eok (萬萬億) place; this is a number of the jo order, with 8,000.
*In the modern Korean number-naming system, 1 jo is $10^{12}$.*

一十即十兆位九萬
10, that is, 90,000 at the tens-of-jo place.

二百即百兆位斤下帶两者用斤
200, that is, the hundreds-of-jo place; amounts that carry ryang (兩) attached below the geun (斤, catty) use the geun as the unit.

八千即千兆位下留两法詳見
筭書
8,000, that is, the thousands-of-jo place. The method of keeping the ryang below is set out in detail in
books of computation (筭書).

二萬即萬兆位
20,000, that is, the ten-thousands-of-jo place.

大數有三等如言千萬曰億十億曰兆十兆曰亰此丁數也如言萬萬曰億萬萬億曰垂萬萬兆曰京此中數也如言萬萬曰億億億曰兆兆兆曰京此上數也
Large numbers have three grades. To call ten million eok (億), ten eok jo (兆), and ten jo gyeong (京) is the lower series (下數). To call wanwan (萬萬) eok, wanwan-eok jo, and wanwan-jo gyeong is the middle series (中數). To call wanwan eok, eok-eok jo, and jo-jo gyeong is the upper series (上數).

下數淺短計有未周上數宏廓用無所適故今以中數紀位
The lower series is shallow and short, so that it falls short of what reckoning requires; the upper series is vast and boundless, so that it suits no practical use. Therefore we now record the places using the middle series.

先㝎位名凢筭數之位左為上而右為下左為首而右為尾生數自右而陞成數自左而降皆以滿十為率三位而㓛百五位而成萬九位而成億十七位而成兆二十工位而成京
First, fix the names of the places. In the places of rod computation (筭數), the left is upper and the right is lower; the left is the head and the right is the tail. Numbers arise by ascending from the right, and numbers are completed by descending from the left, in all cases taking the filling of ten as the rule. At three places [a number] becomes a hundred (百); at five places it becomes ten thousand (萬); at nine places it becomes eok (億); at seventeen places it becomes jo (兆); and at twenty-five places it becomes gyeong (京).

筭法莫先扵正名莫要扵㝎位苟或不審乎定位則以千為百以寸為尺者多矣傳曰差若毫釐繆千里此之謂也
In the art of computation, nothing comes before rectifying names, and nothing is more essential than fixing the places. If one is not careful in fixing the places, then many will mistake a thousand for a hundred, or a chon (寸) for a chi (尺). As the transmitted saying goes, "a difference of a hair's breadth becomes an error of a thousand li" — this is exactly what is meant.

This commentary on the units of numbers is written in connection with Chapter 2.

### Comparison Table of the Three Grades of Large Numbers (Lower, Middle, and Upper Series)

A comparison of the three-tier structure of the lower series (下數), middle series (中數), and upper series (上數) presented in the large-number (大數) naming system, and of the mathematical expression $f(x)$ of each progression ($x$ is the unit-step index, $x = 0$: man, $x = 1$: eok, $x = 2$: jo, $x = 3$: gyeong ...), is as follows.

Category | Increase rule | Formula f(x) | man (萬) | eok (億) | jo (兆) | gyeong (京) | Remarks
-- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | --
Lower series (下數) | increases tenfold with each change of number name | $f(x)=10^(4+x)$ | 10⁴ | 10⁵ | 10⁶ | 10⁷ | 淺短計有未周: the range is narrow and falls short for computation
Middle series (中數) | 10⁴-fold from man to eok, then 10⁸-fold at each step | f(0)=10⁴, f(x)=10^(8x) (x≥1) | 10⁴ | 10⁸(萬萬) | 10¹⁶(萬萬億) | 10²⁴(萬萬兆) | the practical number-naming system adopted in the main text
Upper series (上數) | increases by squaring the value of the preceding unit | f(0)=10⁴, f(x)=f(x−1)² | 10⁴ | 10⁸(萬萬) | 10¹⁶(億億) | 10³²(兆兆) | 宏廓用無所適: the range is excessively large, unsuitable for practical use

The middle and upper series have the same values up through man, eok, and jo, but they diverge from gyeong onward. In the middle series, gyeong is wanwan-jo (萬萬兆), i.e. 10²⁴; in the upper series, gyeong is jo-jo (兆兆), i.e. 10³².

### Significance

This provides insight into the proper establishment of numerical notation and a system of units.

Even by the modern SI system, writing 10 kg of rice as 10,000 g makes the quantity harder to grasp.
If it is written in milligrams as 10,000,000 mg, language struggles even more to describe the amount.
But conversely, expressing the same thing as 0.01 t makes the quantity appear far smaller than it actually is.

Compared with the old lower, middle, and upper series, using the tonne as the unit for a sack of rice is akin to using the upper series, while using the milligram for rice is close to using the lower series. A sack of rice is therefore something that must be expressed in a suitable unit, neither too large nor too small.

This lesson is already known in many cultures, which use scientific notation instead of a lower, middle, and upper series.

In this book's middle-series naming system, 1 eok is $1\text{e}+8$ and 1 jo is $1\text{e}+16$. This makes it visually apparent that 1 jo is $10^8$ times 1 eok, but it is difficult to gauge the quantity intuitively. This is the same characteristic as the upper series.

0.0001 is $10^{-4}$, hence $1\text{e}-4$. And 0.00000001 is $10^{-8}$, hence $1\text{e}-8$.

This likewise makes it visually apparent that 0.00000001 is $10^{-4}$ times — that is, ten thousand times — smaller than 0.0001. Yet it is hard to tell how small a number that is; it is usable in sciences such as chemistry and physics, but not something to be used in natural history and the like, which deal with ordinary objects.

This is a practical example that fully explains why the uses of the upper, middle, and lower series are separated.
