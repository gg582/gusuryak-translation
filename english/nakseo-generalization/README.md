# Nakseo Generalization: the Exact Common Principle of Complement Pairs

## The common rule

The common denominator is not merely a constant sum. It is an **equivariant expansion** between a positional involution and a value-complement involution:

\[
\tau:P\to P,\qquad\kappa_S(x)=S-x,\qquad v\circ\tau=\kappa_S\circ v.
\]

This is equivalent to `v(p)+v(τp)=S` for every position. The standard 3×3 Lo Shu is the smallest example: opposite entries sum to 10, while the central 5 is the sole fixed point.

For 洛書六觚圖, `通加洛書數六倍` lifts `k↔10-k` to `6k↔6(10-k)`, and `54+6=60` is the first such pair. After the central vacancy, the 270 positions form 135 antipodal orbits and values 1…270 form 135 `κ_271`-orbits. Therefore, if the author applied this equivariant Lo Shu extension principle, antipodal sums of 271 are mathematically forced.

This does not turn the rule into a direct transcription: without equivariance, the geometry and aggregate sums do not imply it. The result is a strong reconstruction principle, supported by the title 洛書六觚圖 and the book-internal 圓束樣式 precedent, rather than a literal placement command.
