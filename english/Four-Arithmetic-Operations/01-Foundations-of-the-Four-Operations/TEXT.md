### Unified Theory of the Four Operations (統論四法)

Unified theory of the four operations: an integrated theory of the four computational methods (the four arithmetic operations)

有四法加減乘除是已加減二法俗云行筭加者

(In computation) there are four methods: addition (加), subtraction (減), multiplication (乘), and division (除) — these are they. The two methods of addition and subtraction are commonly called "reckoning with rods (行筭)". Addition means

添而息之減者損而消之加為陽減為陰加法以減

adding to increase; subtraction means taking away to diminish. Addition belongs to yang (陽) and subtraction belongs to yin (陰), so the method of addition is restored to its original state (checked) by subtracting,

還原減法以加還原即一格筭也乘者二數相交而

and the method of subtraction is restored to its original state by adding — this is a single-stage reckoning (a first-order operation). Multiplication means that two numbers cross with each other and

生出所乘之數除者二數相準而約得所除之數乘

produce the multiplied number; division means that two numbers are weighed against each other and reduced, yielding the divided number. Multiplication

亦陽除亦陰乘法以除還原除法以乘還原即多格

likewise belongs to yang and division likewise belongs to yin, so the method of multiplication is restored to its original state by dividing, and the method of division is restored to its original state by multiplying — this is a multi-stage

筭也加減者無法之乘除筭術之權輿也乘除者有

reckoning (a higher-order operation). Addition and subtraction are multiplication and division without fixed rules, the very beginnings of the art of reckoning; multiplication and division are

法之加減筭術之型範也傳曰斷木為棋院革為鞠

addition and subtraction with fixed rules, the standard model of the art of reckoning. Tradition says: "Cut wood to make Go stones; bind leather to make a ball" —

亦皆有法焉况於望術乏精微乎

even these all have their rules; how much more so for the refined and subtle art of reckoning!

## Commentary

This section does not list the four arithmetic operations as four independent calculations; instead, it divides them into two pairs of operations that can each undo the other.

| Operation pair | Forward | Reverse | Restoration to the original state |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Addition and subtraction (加減) | 加 (add) | 減 (subtract) | Subtracting the number that was added returns the original number. |
| Addition and subtraction (加減) | 減 (subtract) | 加 (add) | Adding back the number that was subtracted returns the original number. |
| Multiplication and division (乘除) | 乘 (multiply) | 除 (divide) | Dividing by the number that was multiplied returns the original number. |
| Multiplication and division (乘除) | 除 (divide) | 乘 (multiply) | Multiplying by the number that divided returns the original number. |

Expressed as equations, this is as follows.

```text
(a + b) - b = a
(a - b) + b = a

(a × b) ÷ b = a
(a ÷ b) × b = a
```

The last two equations hold within the range where division is defined and no remainder arises.

### 1. Addition and Subtraction

The main text defines addition as an operation that augments a number to increase it, and subtraction as an operation that removes from a number to decrease it.

| Original text | Modern expression |
| ------ | --------------------------------- |
| 加者添而息之 | Addition appends another number to the given number, increasing its value. |
| 減者損而消之 | Subtraction removes another number from the given number, decreasing its value. |
| 加法以減還原 | Subtracting the same number from the result of an addition restores the original number. |
| 減法以加還原 | Adding the same number to the result of a subtraction restores the original number. |

Here, `還原` (huánwŏn, "return to the origin") means recovering the initial value by applying the opposite operation.

For example, if adding 5 to 7 gives 12, one can verify that subtracting 5 from 12 yields 7.

```text
7 + 5 = 12
12 - 5 = 7
```

Conversely, if subtracting 5 from 12 gives 7, one can verify that adding 5 to 7 yields 12.

```text
12 - 5 = 7
7 + 5 = 12
```

Thus, addition and subtraction perform the same change between two numbers in opposite directions.

| Starting value | Amount of change | Addition | Subtraction |
| --: | --: | ----: | ----: |
|   a |   b | a + b | a - b |
|   7 |   5 |    12 |     2 |
|  12 |   5 |    17 |     7 |

The main text classifies addition as `陽` (yang) and subtraction as `陰` (yin). In calculation, these can be understood as a correspondence representing the increasing direction and the decreasing direction, respectively.

### 2. Multiplication and Division

Multiplication and division are likewise explained as a pair of mutually opposite operations.

| Original text | Modern expression |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| 乘者二數相交而生出所乘之數 | Multiplication combines two numbers to obtain a product. |
| 除者二數相準而約得所除之數 | Division compares two numbers to obtain a quotient. |
| 乘法以除還原 | Dividing the result of a multiplication by the same number restores the original number. |
| 除法以乘還原 | Multiplying the result of a division by the same number restores the original number. |

For example, if multiplying 7 by 5 gives 35, dividing 35 by 5 restores 7.

```text
7 × 5 = 35
35 ÷ 5 = 7
```

If dividing 35 by 5 gives 7, multiplying 7 by 5 restores 35.

```text
35 ÷ 5 = 7
7 × 5 = 35
```

Multiplication and division do not change a number by a fixed amount; they change it according to a fixed multiple.

| Starting value | Reference value | Multiplication | Division |
| --: | --: | ----: | ----: |
|   a |   b | a × b | a ÷ b |
|   7 |   5 |    35 |   7/5 |
|  35 |   5 |   175 |     7 |

Whereas addition and subtraction handle differences between numbers, multiplication and division handle ratios between numbers.

### 3. The Structure of the Two Operation Pairs

The main text groups addition and multiplication together as operations in one direction, and subtraction and division as operations in the opposite direction.

| Basic change | Repeated / multiplicative change | Opposite operation |
| ----- | -------- | ------------- |
| 加 (add) | 乘 (multiply) | Increases a number. |
| 減 (subtract) | 除 (divide) | Decreases or partitions a number. |

However, addition and multiplication are not the same operation.

```text
a + b
```

is an operation that increases a by b, whereas

```text
a × b
```

is an operation that makes a into b times itself.

For example, when the starting value is 4 and the reference value is 3, the results are as follows.

| Operation | Calculation | Result |
| --- | ----: | --: |
| Addition | 4 + 3 |   7 |
| Multiplication | 4 × 3 |  12 |
| Subtraction | 4 - 3 |   1 |
| Division | 4 ÷ 3 | 4/3 |

Addition and subtraction directly append or remove a fixed amount. Multiplication and division change one number according to its multiplicative relationship with another number.

### 4. Single-Row Calculation (一格算) and Multi-Row Calculation (多格算)

The main text distinguishes addition and subtraction as `一格算` (single-row calculation) and multiplication and division as `多格算` (multi-row calculation).

| Category | Corresponding operations | Calculation structure |
| --- | ----- | --------------------------- |
| 一格算 (single-row) | 加·減 | Directly adds or subtracts the digits in corresponding places. |
| 多格算 (multi-row) | 乘·除 | Computes using multiple places and multiple intermediate results. |

Single-digit addition immediately gives the sum of two digits.

```text
7 + 2 = 9
```

Multi-digit addition likewise processes each place in turn.

```text
  347
+ 286
-----
  633
```

By contrast, multiplication combines each digit of one number with each digit of the other in turn, and arranges the intermediate results according to place value.

```text
   347
×   26
------
  2082
 6940
------
 9022
```

Division likewise repeats the procedure of setting a quotient digit, multiplying, and subtracting, several times over.

Thus, `一格算` and `多格算` can be read not as indicating the mathematical degree of an operation, but as expressions distinguishing the number of places and steps handled in the course of a calculation.

### 5. Multiplication and Division without a Rule (無法之乘除) and Addition and Subtraction with a Rule (有法之加減)

The main text summarizes the relationship between addition/subtraction and multiplication/division as follows.

| Original text | Modern expression |
| -------- | --------------------------------------- |
| 加減者無法之乘除 | Addition and subtraction are the basic form of multiplication and division, not yet organized into a rule of repetition. |
| 乘除者有法之加減 | Multiplication and division are operations that perform addition and subtraction repeatedly according to a fixed rule. |
| 算術之權輿也 | Addition and subtraction are the starting point of arithmetic calculation. |
| 算術之型範也 | Multiplication and division are the basic pattern by which multi-step calculation procedures are constructed. |

Multiplication can be expanded as repeated addition of the same number.

```text
4 × 3
= 4 + 4 + 4
= 12
```

Division can be expanded as repeated subtraction of the same number.

```text
12 ÷ 3
= 4

12 - 3 = 9
 9 - 3 = 6
 6 - 3 = 3
 3 - 3 = 0
```

Since subtracting 3 four times yields 0, the quotient is 4.

Actual multiplication and division, however, are not carried out by simple repetition alone. Place value and intermediate results are used to reduce the number of repetitions. Thus `有法` ("having a rule") can be understood as organizing addition and subtraction according to a fixed procedure.

The relationships among the four operations can be summarized as follows.

```text
addition ── opposite operation ── subtraction
   │                              │
   │ repetition, place value      │ repetition, place value
   ↓                              ↓
multiplication ── opposite operation ── division
```

|    3 |    7 | 10 |
|    4 |    6 | 10 |
|    5 |    5 | 10 |
|    6 |    4 | 10 |
|    7 |    3 | 10 |
|    8 |    2 | 10 |
|    9 |    1 | 10 |

For each number, the partner that makes 10 can be found as follows.

```text
10 - a
```

|  a | 10 - a |
| -: | -----: |
|  1 |      9 |
|  2 |      8 |
|  3 |      7 |
|  4 |      6 |
|  5 |      5 |
|  6 |      4 |
|  7 |      3 |
|  8 |      2 |
|  9 |      1 |

This correspondence is used when handling carries in single-digit addition.

For example, when adding 7 to 8, 8 needs 2 to become 10. Splitting 7 into 2 and 5 gives the following.

```text
8 + 7
= 8 + 2 + 5
= 10 + 5
= 15
```

In the same way, adding 8 to 6 gives the following.

```text
6 + 8
= 6 + 4 + 4
= 10 + 4
= 14
```

Thus the addition pairs that make 10 serve as the standard for decomposing additions that involve a carry.

### 8. Carrying

When a sum exceeds a single digit, the main text handles the current place and the next higher place separately.

| Original text | Handling |
| ------- | ---------------------------- |
| 盈者本位見成數 | In the current place, record the number that remains after removing 10. |
| 進位得一數   | Add 1 to the next higher place. |

Let S be the sum of the single-digit numbers a and b.

```text
S = a + b
```

If S is less than 10, record S as it is in the current place.

```text
7 + 2 = 9
```

If S is 10 or more, remove 10 from the current place and carry 1 to the next higher place.

```text
8 + 7 = 15
```

Broken down by place, this is as follows.

| Item | Value |
| ----------- | -: |
| Total sum | 15 |
| Number remaining in the current place |  5 |
| Number carried up to the next place |  1 |

In general, single-digit addition can be handled as follows.

```text
current place = the number remaining after removing 10 from (a + b)
carry         = 1 if (a + b) is 10 or more, otherwise 0
```

Within the range of numbers presented in the main text, both a and b run from 1 to 9, so the sum is at most 18. Therefore the carry arising from a single addition is either 0 or 1.

| Range of a + b | Current place | Carry |
| --------- | ---------: | -: |
| 0 to 9    |      a + b |  0 |
| 10 to 18  | a + b - 10 |  1 |

In multi-digit addition, the carry arising in a lower place is added into the next place.

```text
  78
+ 67
----
```

Ones place:

```text
8 + 7 = 15
```

Record 5 in the current place and carry 1 to the tens place.

Tens place:

```text
7 + 6 + 1 = 14
```

Record 4 in the current place and carry 1 to the hundreds place.

The result is as follows.

```text
  78
+ 67
----
 145
```

### 9. The Single-Digit Subtraction Table

The subtraction table in the main text enumerates the single-digit subtractions that leave a positive result.

|  − |  1 |  2 |  3 |  4 |  5 |  6 |  7 |  8 |
| -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: | -: |
|  9 |  8 |  7 |  6 |  5 |  4 |  3 |  2 |  1 |
|  8 |  7 |  6 |  5 |  4 |  3 |  2 |  1 |    |
|  7 |  6 |  5 |  4 |  3 |  2 |  1 |    |    |
|  6 |  5 |  4 |  3 |  2 |  1 |    |    |    |
|  5 |  4 |  3 |  2 |  1 |    |    |    |    |
|  4 |  3 |  2 |  1 |    |    |    |    |    |
|  3 |  2 |  1 |    |    |    |    |    |    |
|  2 |  1 |    |    |    |    |    |    |    |

This table covers only the following range.

```text
minuend > subtrahend
```

Therefore, cases where the result is 0 or becomes negative are not included in the table.

For example, the following equations are in the table.

```text
9 - 8 = 1
7 - 4 = 3
5 - 2 = 3
```

However, the following equations are not recorded.

```text
5 - 5 = 0
3 - 7 = -4
```

The table in this section is used as a basic subtraction table for finding positive differences within the range of natural numbers.

### 10. The Correspondence between the Addition and Subtraction Tables

The addition table and the subtraction table read the same numerical relationships in opposite directions.

| Addition    | Corresponding subtraction |
| --------- | --------- |
| 1 + 8 = 9 | 9 - 8 = 1 |
| 2 + 7 = 9 | 9 - 7 = 2 |
| 3 + 6 = 9 | 9 - 6 = 3 |
| 4 + 5 = 9 | 9 - 5 = 4 |
| 3 + 4 = 7 | 7 - 4 = 3 |
| 2 + 3 = 5 | 5 - 3 = 2 |

From a single equation

```text
a + b = c
```

the following two subtraction equations can be obtained.

```text
c - a = b
c - b = a
```

For example, suppose the following addition equation is given.

```text
4 + 5 = 9
```

Then the following two subtraction equations hold.

```text
9 - 4 = 5
9 - 5 = 4
```

Thus the addition table and the subtraction table are not separate tables of results, but tables that look up the relationship among the same three numbers in opposite directions.

### 11. The Overall Structure of the Calculation Procedures

The content presented in this section can be organized by order of calculation as follows.

#### Addition

1. Add the digits in the two places.
2. If the sum is less than 10, record it as it is in the current place.
3. If the sum is 10 or more, record the remainder after removing 10 in the current place.
4. Add 1 to the next higher place.
5. Repeat up to the highest place.

#### Subtraction

1. Subtract the subtrahend from the minuend in the current place.
2. If subtraction is possible, record the difference in the current place.
3. If subtraction is not possible in the current place, borrow 1 from the next higher place.
4. Convert the borrowed 1 into 10 in the current place and perform the subtraction.
5. Repeat up to the highest place.

#### Multiplication

1. Use single-digit addition as the basic operation.
2. Compute the repetition of the same number or the partial products of each digit.
3. Arrange the intermediate results according to place value.
4. Combine the intermediate results by addition.

#### Division

1. Use single-digit subtraction and multiplication as the basic operations.
2. Determine how many times the divisor goes into the current partial value.
3. Subtract the product of that quotient digit and the divisor from the current partial value.
4. Bring down the next digit and repeat the same process.

The handling of place values mentioned in the main text makes this more precise.

```text
  102
-   7
-----
   95
```

In this case, `2-7` goes negative, so the value is insufficient.
At this point, 10 is borrowed from the hundreds place to make it 90, and because the borrowed tens place is one place higher than 7, the borrowed amount is reduced to 9 according to the (9+1)=10 addition table, and it moves down one column. The 10 reaches the ones place where the 2 is, and 12-7=5. After the borrowing process, 90 remains in front, and 90+5=95.

Thus this section organizes the four operations into the following dependency relationships.

```text
single-digit addition table ─┬─ multi-digit addition
                             └─ partial calculations of multiplication

single-digit subtraction table ─┬─ multi-digit subtraction
                                └─ partial calculations of division

addition ↔ subtraction
multiplication ↔ division
```

Addition and subtraction are the basic operation pair used directly in calculation, while multiplication and division are the operation pair that combines addition/subtraction with place-value handling according to a fixed procedure.

### 0. The Problem of Infinite Decimals

When 1 is divided by each of the natural numbers from 2 to 9, some results are infinite decimals and some are not.

In the main text, division by 2, 4, 5, or 8 yields a finite decimal, while division by 3, 6, 7, or 9 yields an infinite decimal (in the original text, 不盡之數, "a number that is not exhausted" — a number whose decimal expansion grows without ever terminating).

Although this passage speaks only of division, infinite decimals can readily arise in other operations as well. For example, taking the square roots of the natural numbers from 2 to 9: 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, and 8 yield infinite decimals, while 4 and 9 yield the natural numbers 2 and 3.

Computers, counting rods (算, sangaji), and the abacus are all ill-suited to computing such infinite decimals. Therefore, deciding at what point to adopt an approximate value can also be regarded as part of one's arithmetical capability.

### 1. The Roles of the Three Numbers in Multiplication

Multiplication is an operation that combines two numbers to obtain a single product.

| Name | Role | Modern expression |
| -- | ---------- | ------ |
| 元數 (origin number) | The number given first | Multiplicand |
| 法數 (rule number) | The number that multiplies the 元數 | Multiplier |
| 積數 (product number) | The result obtained by multiplication | Product |

The basic form of multiplication is as follows.

```text
元數 × 法數 = 積數
```

For example, in

```text
3 × 9 = 27
```

the role of each number is as follows.

|  Number | Name | Role |
| -: | -- | --------- |
|  3 | 元數 | The number given first |
|  9 | 法數 | The multiplying number |
| 27 | 積數 | The result obtained by multiplying |

The main text's `乘數夫元而妻法夫乘妻而求積也` explains the structure of combining the 元數 and the 法數 to obtain the 積數 by analogy with the husband-wife relationship. For calculation, it suffices to take only the following relationship, rather than the metaphor.

```text
3 × 9 = 27
```

### 2. The Roles of the Three Numbers in Division

Division is an operation that uses the result of a multiplication together with one of the two factors to find the remaining factor.

| Name | Role | Modern expression |
| -- | ----------- | ------ |
| 實數 (dividend number) | The number being divided | Dividend |
| 法數 (rule number) | The dividing number | Divisor |
| 商數 (quotient number) | The result obtained by division | Quotient |

The basic form of division is as follows.

```text
實數 ÷ 法數 = 商數
```

For example, in

```text
27 ÷ 3 = 9
```

the role of each number is as follows.

|  Number | Name | Role |
| -: | -- | ------- |
| 27 | 實數 | The number being divided |
|  3 | 法數 | The dividing number |
|  9 | 商數 | The quotient obtained |

The main text's `除數母法而子實母除子而求商也` explains the structure of dividing the 實數 by the 法數 to obtain the 商數 by analogy with the mother-child relationship.

```text
27 ÷ 3 = 9
```

### 3. Finding the Unknown in a Multiplication Equation

In the multiplication equation

```text
3 × y = 27
```

`y` corresponds to the 法數.

| Term | Value |
| -- | -: |
| 元數 |  3 |
| 法數 |  y |
| 積數 | 27 |

To find the 法數, divide the 積數 by the 元數.

```text
法數 = 積數 ÷ 元數
```

Substituting the numerical values gives the following.

```text
y = 27 ÷ 3
y = 9
```

Therefore,

```text
3 × 9 = 27
```

### 4. The Two Division Equations Obtained from a Single Multiplication Equation

Each of the two factors used in a multiplication equation can serve as the divisor for finding the other factor.

```text
元數 × 法數 = 積數
```

From this, the following two division equations arise.

```text
積數 ÷ 元數 = 法數
積數 ÷ 法數 = 元數
```

Applied to `3 × 9 = 27`, this gives the following.

| Number sought | Calculation | Result |
| ------ | -----: | -: |
| 法數     | 27 ÷ 3 |  9 |
| 元數     | 27 ÷ 9 |  3 |

Thus a single multiplication equation is grouped together with the two corresponding division equations as follows.

| Operation | Equation |
| --- | ---------: |
| Multiplication | 3 × 9 = 27 |
| Division | 27 ÷ 3 = 9 |
| Division | 27 ÷ 9 = 3 |

In general, if three numbers `a`, `b`, `c` satisfy the relationship

```text
a × b = c
```

then the following relationships also hold together with it.

```text
c ÷ a = b
c ÷ b = a
```

### 5. The Change of Names According to the Operation

The same number bears different names depending on the role it plays in multiplication and in division.

Comparing `3 × 9 = 27` with `27 ÷ 3 = 9` gives the following.

|  Number | Name in multiplication | Name in division |
| -: | -------- | --------- |
|  3 | 元數       | 法數        |
|  9 | 法數       | 商數        |
| 27 | 積數       | 實數        |

The `積數`, which is the result of multiplication, becomes the `實數`, the number being divided, when division begins.

```text
積數 → 實數
```

Of the two factors in a multiplication, the known number becomes the `法數` of the division, and the remaining factor becomes the `商數` sought by the division.

```text
known factor   → 法數
factor sought  → 商數
```

Therefore, the names `元數` and `法數` are not names fixed to the numbers themselves, but indicate the role each number plays in the current calculation.

### 6. 元數亦稱實數 (The Origin Number Is Also Called the Dividend Number)

`元數亦稱實數` means that the 元數 is also called the 實數. This does not mean that the 元數 and the 實數 always occupy the same position; it is an explanation of nomenclature, to the effect that the original number serving as the basis of a calculation can also be called the 實數 depending on the context.

Still, when distinguishing the roles in multiplication and division, it is clearer to use the terms separately as follows.

| Operation | Initially given value | Basis of operation | Result |
| --- | -------- | ----- | -- |
| Multiplication | 元數       | 法數    | 積數 |
| Division | 實數       | 法數    | 商數 |

### 7. The Mutual Conversion between Multiplication and Division

The relationship between multiplication and division can be summarized in the following table.

| Known numbers | Number sought | Calculation |
| ------- | ------ | ------- |
| 元數, 法數  | 積數     | 元數 × 法數 |
| 積數, 元數  | 法數     | 積數 ÷ 元數 |
| 積數, 法數  | 元數     | 積數 ÷ 法數 |

For example, if the three numbers are `3`, `9`, and `27`, then the following holds.

| Known numbers | Number sought | Equation | Result |
| ------- | ------ | -----: | -: |
| 3, 9    | Product |  3 × 9 | 27 |
| 27, 3   | Other factor | 27 ÷ 3 |  9 |
| 27, 9   | Other factor | 27 ÷ 9 |  3 |

Thus the computational core of this paragraph is the following three equations.

```text
元數 × 法數 = 積數
積數 ÷ 元數 = 法數
積數 ÷ 法數 = 元數
```

This is also connected to the principle of reading the multiplication table in reverse as a division table.

| Multiplication table | Corresponding division table |
| ---------- | ---------- |
| 3 × 9 = 27 | 27 ÷ 3 = 9 |
| 3 × 8 = 24 | 24 ÷ 3 = 8 |
| 3 × 7 = 21 | 21 ÷ 3 = 7 |
| 3 × 6 = 18 | 18 ÷ 3 = 6 |
| 3 × 5 = 15 | 15 ÷ 3 = 5 |
| 3 × 4 = 12 | 12 ÷ 3 = 4 |
| 3 × 3 = 9  | 9 ÷ 3 = 3  |
| 3 × 2 = 6  | 6 ÷ 3 = 2  |
| 3 × 1 = 3  | 3 ÷ 3 = 1  |

In other words, the division table (division gu-gu) of the main text is not a newly enumerated set of separate results, but a table arranged so that the 積數 of the multiplication table (gu-gu) is taken as the 實數 and the 元數 as the 法數, so as to find the 商數 in reverse.
